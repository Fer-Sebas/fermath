# Overview
The null space of a [linear transformation](Linear%20Transformation) from one vector space to another, is the **subset of the first vector space** consisting of **all vectors that the transformation maps to zero** in the codomain.
<br><br>
$$\Large null(T) = \{\space v \in V \space |\space T(v) = 0_W\space \}$$
<br><br>
The null space captures **what gets collapsed to nothing** by the transformation. 

If a vector lies in the null space, applying $\large T$ produces no effect. The transformation cannot distinguish that vector from the zero vector.

The null space lives entirely in the **domain** $\large V$, not the codomain $\large W$.

The null space is always a [subspace](subspace) of $\large V$. This makes the null space a geometric and algebraic object that measures how much information the transformation lose.
